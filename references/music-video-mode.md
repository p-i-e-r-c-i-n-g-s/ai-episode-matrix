# Music-video mode

Music-video mode is Episode mode compressed into a short, rhythm-led form. Continuity still matters, but the unit of planning is the beat, lyric, hook, or musical phrase rather than a long scene.

Treat the audio file or track brief as a first-class source asset. Record its version, tempo/markers, sections, lyrics or transcript, and rights/usage status before designing shots. If timing is unknown, label beat sync as `[UNRESOLVED]` rather than inventing it.

## Required decisions

- Track, tempo, beat/lyric markers, and emotional arc, if supplied.
- Performance, narrative, atmosphere, or mixed coverage.
- Cut-driven montage or one-take/continuous choreography.
- A small visual vocabulary: recurring color, prop, gesture, location, transition, or motif.
- What must remain continuous across shots: performer identity, wardrobe, set, lighting, and movement direction.
- Whether on-screen lyrics, subtitles, typography, or no text are wanted; text is a designed layer, not automatic caption cleanup.

## Compact shot contract

```text
BEAT / TIME: [musical or lyric marker]
PURPOSE: [performance | narrative | transition | atmosphere]
PRESERVE: [reference-image and continuity locks]
ACTION: [one readable performance or story beat]
CAMERA / CUT: [movement, shot length, cut point, transition]
MOTIF: [repeated visual element and variation]
AUDIO: [track/lyric sync; no invented lyric]
AVOID: [identity drift, random cuts, unsynced gestures, continuity breaks]
```

Use shorter shot durations and more coverage than ordinary Episode mode, but do not put multiple unrelated actions into one generation. For a one-take concept, replace cut points with spatial choreography and maintain a clear start/end state.

Use four gates: audio/lyrics timing confirmed; visual and asset plan approved; each generated shot reviewed; final edit checked for beat alignment, lyric readability, continuity, audio clipping, and export integrity. Keep an edit decision list with shot IDs, in/out time, beat/lyric marker, transition, and approval state.

## Supplied grammar profiles

The supplied JSON provides useful starting points: glossy fisheye performance, deadpan magical realism, meta/vintage television, gothic decay, tactile dreamscape, continuous tracking choreography, mixed media/rotoscope, and intimate solo performance. Translate a profile into observable choices; do not copy a named video, protected performer, lyrics, or choreography.
