# Cinematography discipline

Use these controls to turn vague cinematic language into visible, physically motivated choices. Pick only what the shot needs.

## Camera

- Translational: lateral track, dolly in/out, pedestal/boom, crane or jib sweep.
- Rotational: slow pan, whip pan, Dutch tilt.
- Rig character: locked tripod, weighted Steadicam, or reactive shoulder handheld.
- State the subject path, camera path, speed, motivation, and end relationship.

## Light and atmosphere

Name the source, direction, quality, temperature, and interaction with air: motivated practicals, hard specular light, soft bounce, rim light, mixed color temperatures, volumetric rays, halation, humidity, or visible cold.

## Blocking and physics

Use readable geography: deep staging, triangular blocking, lateral crossing, parallax, screen direction, and eyelines. Add cause-and-effect secondary motion: follow-through in hair/props, fabric weight, reactive crowds, dust, rain, or reflections.

## Optics and capture

Choose a focal-length effect rather than stacking numbers: ultra-wide spatial exaggeration, standard natural perspective, or telephoto compression. Choose shallow or deep focus, rack focus, and a shutter character only when it changes the result. Keep capture format, lens, emulsion influence, presentation, and cinematic grammar separate.

## Director translation

Translate a director into a small set of observable constraints: composition, camera, light, blocking, pacing, production design, and sound-image relationship. Never use a name as the complete prompt. Profiles from the supplied director JSON are available in [director-movie-modes.md](director-movie-modes.md); use them as inspiration for original work, not copied scenes or protected characters.
